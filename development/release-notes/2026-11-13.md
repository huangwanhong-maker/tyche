# Release Notes: 13 November 2026

## 1. Lifetimes, Bayesian Computation, and Uncertainty

In this update tyche can say how long things last, draw from a posterior known only up to its constant, and carry uncertainty through a model to say which inputs it answers to. These serve the infrastructure's industrial ecology domain (how long buildings and products stay in use) and the calibration of its models to records.

- **Lifetime laws** (decision 0020): the exponential, Weibull and lognormal laws, each with its density, distribution, survival, hazard and quantiles, and given by a mean and a spread when that is what is known. 16 checks.
- **Bayesian computation** (decision 0021):
  - conjugate models: beta–binomial, gamma–Poisson, the normal with a known variance or with a normal–inverse-gamma prior, and Dirichlet–multinomial, each with its posterior and predictive law;
  - adaptive Metropolis and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo with a step size tuned by dual averaging, on any log-density, several chains at once;
  - diagnostics: split R̂ and the effective sample size by Geyer's initial sequence, which flag chains that disagree.
  - 25 checks: posteriors by hand, normal laws recovered across scales and correlations, the effective size of an autoregression against its closed form.
- **Uncertainty and sensitivity** (decision 0022): Monte Carlo and Latin hypercube sampling, propagation through a model, the delta method, Morris's screening by elementary effects, and Sobol' indices of the first order and total, with bootstrap intervals. Ishigami's function gives back its known indices. 15 checks.

## 2. Compositions and Relational Data

In this update tyche reads data that are parts of a whole, and data that are ties among units. Both serve the infrastructure's sociology (a day of 1440 minutes; networks of people), and any work with such data: budgets, rocks, microbiomes, trade between places.

- **Compositions** (decision 0018):
  - Aitchison's geometry of the simplex: closure, perturbation and powering, log-ratio coordinates, distances;
  - balances and pivot coordinates from a partition of the parts;
  - the centre and total variation of a sample, and the variation matrix;
  - zeros replaced below the limits one declares;
  - compositions as predictors in linear models, with what an outcome does when time moves one-for-one, or one-for-the-rest, between parts; and compositions as responses.
- **Relational data** (decision 0019):
  - density, reciprocity, and the censuses of dyads and triads;
  - transitivity, clustering, components and cores;
  - assortativity by degree and by attribute, the E–I index against its baseline, and inbreeding homophily;
  - Burt's constraint and effective size;
  - QAP and MRQAP by permutation, and conditional uniform graph tests that hold the degrees.
- **Checks.** Every result is held to closed forms, identities and brute force; the networks are also held to networkx on Zachary's karate club, as a cross-check. 239 checks.

## 3. Classes in a Sample, and Communities

In this update tyche estimates how many kinds a population holds, how evenly they are filled, and what a larger sample would find. It also compares communities of counts. This serves the infrastructure's ecology domain, and any work that counts kinds: vocabularies, libraries of sequences, canons.

- **Diversity of a law:** Hill numbers of any order with their limits, Rényi's entropy, and diversity that knows how alike the classes are.
- **Diversity among units:** Jost's partition into α and β, and the overlaps between units.
- **From a sample of individuals:**
  - richness by Chao1 with its interval, and ACE;
  - the sample's coverage;
  - asymptotic estimates of orders 0, 1 and 2;
  - rarefaction and extrapolation at any size, curves with bootstrap bands, and the size at which a coverage is reached.
- **From incidences** (quadrats, visits): Chao2 and the richness curve.
- **Communities of counts:** dissimilarities (Bray–Curtis, Jaccard, Sørensen, Hellinger, chord); principal coordinates with their corrections; PERMANOVA with exact or permuted p-values; the homogeneity of dispersions; Mantel's test.
- Every formula is held to brute force, identities or simulation, and to scikit-bio as a cross-check where it has the estimator.
