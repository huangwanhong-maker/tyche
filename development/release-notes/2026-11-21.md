# Release Notes: 21 November 2026

## Where a Network's Nodes Lie, and What of It Can Be Said

`networks` can now place a network's nodes in a space from its adjacency, and says what of the placement is identified ([decision 0033](../decisions/0033-latent-positions-with-their-group.md)): the probabilities always; distances only when the network joins like nodes more than unlike ones, since otherwise a hyperbolic rotation moves them. A statistic is tested against the group before it is reported, two placements of the same nodes are aligned by Procrustes, the dimension is chosen from the scree, and the rows of a bipartite incidence are ordered as the economic complexity index orders them, which is what that index is.

## The Order of Two Questions

When two questions are asked in two orders, `testing` can now say whether the order changed the answers and whether the change is of the kind quantum-like models of judgement allow ([decision 0032](../decisions/0032-the-order-of-two-questions.md)). It fits the classical models (no order effect; each order its own) and a projective one, nested, and tests the QQ equality, which needs no parameter; and since not rejecting the equality is not evidence for it, it can show it within a declared margin by two one-sided tests. A classical model in which the first answer moves the second breaks the equality; a symmetric anchoring keeps it, so the equality alone singles out nothing.

## Forecasts Compared

`time_series` can now say whether one forecast is more accurate than another ([decision 0031](../decisions/0031-forecasts-compared.md)): rolling one-step forecasts from any forecaster, the three that need no model (the random walk, the historical mean and a fitted first-order autoregression), Newey and West's long-run variance on its own, and Diebold and Mariano's test of equal accuracy with the small-sample correction of Harvey, Leybourne and Newbold, which keeps its size at 5% in runs of fifty.
