# 2026-11-16

## Information Bought under a Budget; Intervals for a Proportion

- **Which tests to run first** ([decision 0026](../decisions/0026-information-bought-under-a-budget-and-intervals-for-a-proportion.md)). Several decisions are to be taken, each with its own states, prior and acts, and before each a test could be run at a cost; the budget does not cover them all. When the decisions are separate, a set of tests is worth the sum of their EVSIs, and the best set within the budget is a knapsack, solved by calx with its certificate. A cost may only use the budget, or be paid out of what the test gains, in which case a test not worth its cost is never run. The checks form the joint problem outright and hold the sum to it.
- **Intervals for a proportion.** Wilson's score interval and Clopper and Pearson's exact interval for k successes in n trials, held to their definitions (the roots of the score equation; the points where the binomial's tails hold α/2 each) and to their closed forms at none and all. Their exact coverage is computed over a grid of true proportions, and Clopper and Pearson's is never below its level. The textbook's interval, which has no width at 0 or n, is not offered.

These serve the infrastructure's evidence in space and time ([ADR 0123](https://github.com/huangwanhong-maker/Noetarium-The-Science-and-Humanity-Infrastructure/blob/main/development/decisions/0123-evidence-in-space-and-time.md)): which places to survey first, and what a kind of report is worth when counted against a reference.
