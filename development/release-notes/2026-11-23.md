# Release Notes: 23 November 2026

## How Well a Score Separates Two Groups

`testing` can now say how often a member of one group outranks a member of another ([decision 0036](../decisions/0036-ranks-of-two-samples.md)): Mann and Whitney's U, which is the area under the curve of a score that is meant to tell the groups apart, with its test when values tie.

## Who Was Selected, What Changed, Who Came and Who Left

tyche can now account exactly for how a population's average changed between two times ([decision 0035](../decisions/0035-census-records-and-the-price-decomposition.md)). A population is given as its members with their weights and a measured character, and who descends from whom: firms in an industry, candidate solutions in a search, or lineages in any evolving population. The change of the average is split into selection (the members that grew were those with the character), transmission (the members themselves changed), entry and exit, by Price's equation, with the change from members that continue split further between groups and within them.

Entrants and exiters can be judged against the survivors, against the average before or against the average of the two times, as the literature on productivity does, and the choice is named since it changes what entry is credited with. Each change is credited to what made it: transmission to the operator, entry to its source (an outside proposal, a model, a person), exit to its reason. Every form is exact, checked in exact fractions on two hundred random populations, and the accounting says what happened, never why.
