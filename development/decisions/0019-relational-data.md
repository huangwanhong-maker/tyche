# 0019. Relational Data

- **Status:** Accepted, provisionally (2026-11-13).
- **Date:** 2026-11-13
- **Decided by:** the infrastructure's ADR 0104, which founds its sociology domain with time use and relations (personal and social networks), and its ADR 0105, which founds a bridge domain of social provision (who helps and cares for whom).

## 1. Context

The sociology domain will describe personal and social networks (who names whom, how dense and reciprocal their ties are, how they close into triangles, whether like ties to like, who brokers between groups) and test hypotheses about them; the domain of social provision will ask the same of networks of care and help among households. The statistics of relational data name no object in nature: the same density, census, assortativity and permutation test serve friendships, trade between firms, co-authorship and gene regulation. By the infrastructure's rule they belong in a foundation, and as statistics (description, mixing measures, tests under relational dependence) in tyche. The graphs themselves are morphe's: tyche uses morphe's combinatorics where it already holds what is needed, and its least squares for regression over dyads.

## 2. Decision

**A new module, `networks`** (namespace `tyche::networks`), using `regression`, calx's `linalg` and `random`, and morphe's `combinatorics`:

- **networks as ties:** directed or undirected, with optional positive weights, given as lists of ties; loops refused, repeated ties merged with their weights summed; adjacency matrices, the underlying graph, induced and ego networks, degrees and strengths;
- **description:** density; reciprocity (arc, dyadic, and Garlaschelli and Loffredo's ρ); the dyad census and the triad census of sixteen classes by Batagelj and Mrvar's algorithm; triangles, local and average clustering, transitivity; weak components (morphe's spanning forest) and strong components (Tarjan); core numbers (Batagelj and Zaveršnik); degree distributions;
- **mixing and homophily:** the mixing matrix and Newman's assortativity by a category, a number or degree (any pair of ends for directed networks, or strengths); Krackhardt and Stern's E–I index with group indices and the complete-network reference; inbreeding homophily in Currarini, Jackson and Pin's statement of Coleman's index;
- **brokerage:** Burt's constraint, effective size and efficiency, for directed and weighted networks;
- **inference with relational dependence:** QAP correlation and MRQAP (Y-permutation, and double semi-partialling after Dekker, Krackhardt and Snijders 2007), with p-values reproducible by seed or exact over all relabellings of small networks; conditional uniform graph tests of any statistic, conditioned on the number of ties (drawn exactly) or on the degrees (by double-edge swaps, their number stated).

Strong components and core numbers name no statistical object and belong by nature to morphe's `combinatorics`; they are written here because morphe was being changed by another session and was not to be touched, and may move there later (an open question of the module), as weak components already come from morphe.

Where the sources leave a choice, it is stated in `docs/glossary.md` §7: undirected ties read as mutual by the censuses; clustering, cores and brokerage on the underlying graph; cores by distinct neighbours (networkx counts in + out degree); the order and names of the triad classes; mixing pairs (each undirected tie both ways); the tail's out-degree against the head's in-degree by default; p-values (b + 1)/(B + 1) with the observed counted (sna's `cug.test` reports b/B); the streams of the permutations and draws.

## 3. Alternatives Considered

- **The sociology domain holding these methods.** Rejected by the rule of reuse: every science with relations asks the same.
- **morphe holding them all.** morphe holds graphs and their combinatorics; densities, censuses as data summaries, assortativity, Burt's measures and permutation tests are statistics of observed relations, and their conventions (divisors, p-values, nulls) are tyche's.
- **nomos holding them.** nomos holds the methods of the social sciences as such; network statistics are used as widely outside them (ecology, genomics, economics of trade), so they sit beneath nomos, which may build on them.
- **Borrowing igraph or networkx.** igraph is C but GPL; networkx is Python. The formulas are written here from the papers, and networkx is a cross-check generated by a committed script.

## 4. Verification

`check_tyche_network_descriptive` (41), `check_tyche_mixing` (28) and `check_tyche_qap` (32) hold the module to closed forms (triangles, stars, complete graphs, directed cycles, two-group constructions, Burt's measures worked by hand), to identities (Newman's 2002 form of degree assortativity as a Pearson correlation, Borgatti's effective size, the theorem of Frisch, Waugh and Lovell for the double semi-partialling statistic), to brute force (the triad classes of all 64 labelled triads and their orbit sizes, the census of every triple, components by transitive closure, cores by peeling, QAP and MRQAP p-values over all 120 relabellings of five actors, the uniformity of G(4, 3) and of swaps over the 70 two-regular graphs on six nodes) and to seeded simulations (QAP's p-values under independence, the false-positive rate of an irrelevant correlated matrix, ρ averaging 0 over random digraphs). networkx 3.7's transitivity, clustering, triangles, core numbers, triad census, components, assortativities, constraint and effective size, on Zachary's karate club and seeded random networks, are cross-checks generated by a committed script; its fast path for the effective size of all nodes, which disagrees with its own node-by-node definition for directed and weighted graphs, is not used.

With decision 0018, tyche's checks rise from 1059 to 1298.

## 5. Consequences

The sociology and social-provision domains can describe and test networks against `tyche::networks` without numerical code of their own. Left for later, and named in the module's open questions: exponential random graph, block and latent space models and models of network change; the triad census's expectations under the U|MAN null; measures across many personal networks; moving strong components and cores to morphe; triangle reversals for directed degree-preserving draws; weighted clustering and generalised cores.
